# Gorilla movement and banana rage

The hub's contributor companions use `clankers.js` for movement and behavior, `agent.js` for the articulated rig, and `scene-hub.js` for terrain, contacts and Ooga capture. Movement fixes apply to signed-in and signed-out play. Banana rage is restricted to signed-out, single-player play; it sends no multiplayer messages.

## Movement corrections

Box departures lost support too early: the support point could leave the top while the body's collision cylinder still overlapped the box's vertical side. The next downward step then collided with the very box it was leaving. Keeping support through the same collision footprint lets the body clear the side before its ordinary fall begins. An additional autonomous check treated the root point leaving a short prop as a roof edge despite valid live support under the body; it repeatedly attempted a fall with zero sideways velocity. That check now preserves valid raised-prop support. This applies to a stopped or turning gorilla as well as one already walking. Destroyed support is still reread from the live solid meshes and hands motion to gravity.

The climbing failure was not simply a lack of forward arm reach. Free climbing committed a candidate body move using wall and body clearance, without the articulated grip preview used by planned rail climbing. The rendered pose could then have no supporting palm, and its final grip check correctly caused a fall. Depth transfers must prove the articulated candidate and keep a supporting hand until the other establishes reachable contact. The final rendered hands and movement preview use the same contact limits. A failed proof holds the valid grip or takes the existing fallback; it must not admit penetration or increase reach globally.

Rage initially had a separate, stricter movement path that rejected ordinary prop steps and treated contact with a supporting pebble as penetration. It now uses the driven ground-step support and collision path, subdividing fast movement into steps of at most 0.1 m. Waypoint completion uses live support height, consumes arrived points without an idle frame, and requires actual movement or turning before clearing a blockage. These changes preserve normal prop traversal without bypassing collision or stuck recovery.

Raging clankers and their captives pass through trees, palms and decorative cave uprights such as banners, lantern posts and chalkboards. Collision sweeps, support, ceilings and obstacle lookahead share the same explicit scenery filter, so a hidden footing or grip check cannot snag the pair. Merged cave dressing tags individual decorative faces; terrain, buildings, bridges, gates and furniture keep their solid shells. Short props retain the clanker's existing step-over support. Grab approach, launch-map validation and the thrown Ooga's flight use the same decorative exemption, while cancellation records only strictly clear standing points. Ordinary characters retain their collision rules. Local sidesteps remain available for other obstructions.

The home island has a strict 18.5 m rage border. Teleport capture requires both a target beyond that radius and feet above the ordinary 1.16 m step height, on an exterior ledge or roof. The actual-height cave column excludes cave interiors, including raised floors. Oogas on ordinary ground, inside caves or at or inside the border keep ordinary running pursuit. Satellite islands retain their local capture behavior. Eligible teleport pursuit never falls back to a jump, climb or walking route. Attempts are at least 0.75 seconds apart, with the existing eight-second hunt deadline and target cooldown.

Before taking ownership, the clanker validates the nearest usable flat home throw pad for both bodies and a standing cancellation point. It teleports underneath the target with its palm attached to the original ankle position and holds that visible pose for 0.4 seconds before returning with the same captive. The temporary wall pose permits rock overlap and has no ground fitting or jump/climb/fall controller. G and throw charge stay held during the pause; throwing cannot start there. Destination-only checks certify the returned held limbs without sweeping them through the island; ordinary continuous collision resumes from their new bounds. The ordinary full-charge swing then launches the Ooga at 45 degrees. A blocked endpoint pauses and retries without starting a high-place jump. Fire, swimming or lifecycle cancellation takes priority; cancellation during the wall visit restores the hunter to its pre-teleport position and the target to its recorded cancellation point.

A refused close grab now measures time against the target's position, independently of the hunter's small slides and turns. After 0.75 seconds beside a stationary target without a grip, the hunter attempts a checked 2.5 m diagonal retreat, preserving its facing to avoid turning into the wall. It leaves a 1.25-second opening before a side approach. Two reapproaches are allowed; a third retreat precedes target rejection and cooldown. An exhausted close approach or failed hill takeoff also backs away before leaving the target. Retreat segments share the pursuit check budget, and live movement keeps ordinary collision and support checks. G stays held and a newly clear contact can still capture; a refused grab never moves or takes control of the Ooga.

Each hunt has an eight-second deadline that moving, replanning and jumping do not renew. If capture has not succeeded, the clanker rejects that target for at least eight seconds and selects the nearest remaining eligible Ooga. Normal rage still selects only its agitators; debug rage selects nearby Oogas. Expiry clears the old wall-search intentions and releases an active climb into the checked fall controller. An existing jump or fall finishes before ground pursuit resumes, unless an eligible cliff target admits a teleport. A successful grab clears the hunt deadline, so it cannot interrupt dragging or the charged throw.

Walking foot-contact fitting now removes the previous frame's leg lift before building the next pose. Previously, repeated corrections accumulated against the damped reset and could pull the legs away from the pelvis beside an obstruction. Pose-preview rollback also restores the saved rig without fitting its support a second time. These are source-confirmed defects; the precise cliff-side arrangement in the reported screenshot has not yet been reproduced.

Autonomous throw clearance measures the released flying limbs once per capture and uses that envelope through the flight proof, including feet below the nominal support point. This replaces the narrower walking cylinder that could miss a hand or foot clipping a hill.

## Rage contract and ownership

`net.state.resolved` distinguishes an account lookup in progress from a known signed-out session. Rage is allowed only when account status is resolved and `net.state.me` is absent. Standalone files, deliberately disabled networking and a confirmed missing account backend can resolve as signed out. An uncertain failed account request remains ineligible. A signed-in page never counts hits or runs autonomous rage capture, even with no other visitor online.

`clanker-rage.js` owns each companion's bounded hit history, expiry, target and hunt/approach/throw phase. `clankers.js` connects its `bananaHit` entry point to the movement controller and cancels rage during incompatible lifecycle changes. `crew.js` identifies actual banana projectile impacts with a per-visit serial; `scene-hub.js` applies damage first, then forwards qualifying impacts. Work shots, tomatoes, melee, misses and visual-only replay never enter this accounting path. Hits combine across Ooga shooters, each projectile counts once per gorilla, and hits older than the rolling 60-second window expire. The hit ring retains each shooter's identity; only Oogas who supplied those 42 hits may become rage targets. Working, sleeping or controlled companions cannot activate rage, and loss of eligibility clears accumulated hits.

The 42nd qualifying hit starts one 60-second interval in simulation time. A hit that reduces health to zero cannot also activate rage. Further impacts deal ordinary damage but neither extend rage nor build a second hit history. Existing health regeneration continues; cancellation does not refill health. The triggering shooter is the first target when eligible and reachable; other shooters in that hit window are the only fallback targets.

`scene-hub.js` owns physical capture: each gorilla has one reusable `capture` record and each Ooga has one `grabbedBy` owner. Rage holds its grab during pursuit, takes the first clear body contact, then holds the throw charge throughout the three-limb run to an inland launch position facing the nearest usable coastline. At that supported stance, it finishes the charge, aims 45 degrees upward and outward, releases the throw click, waits for the swing to launch the Ooga, then releases and reengages the grab for the next hunt. A blocked approach or launch selects another stance while retaining the captive and charged throw. Existing player-controlled grabs keep their riding-on-the-gorilla rule. Rage pursues on four limbs at 12 m/s, compared with the ordinary 5.4 m/s run, then reuses the one-handed three-limb drag, charged throw animation and the Ooga's ordinary airborne/abyss recovery. The visitor keeps the same Ooga and camera; capture suppresses conflicting movement and actions until release. The final animated palm and captive pose are previewed before grounded movement commits. A blocked pose holds the previous position while a local movement retry runs; lifecycle cancellation still releases at the last checked standing point.

While rage holds G, eligible targets cannot use that gorilla as a walking support, including during a target switch. Contact is checked again after the gorilla’s movement and pose, before the crew chooses its ground. A blocked initial grip preserves the target’s movement and actions. The approach tries bounded closer and side stances through the normal movement checks. Captive collision sweeps use each limb’s source and destination dimensions; grounded movement estimates follow the captive’s floor rather than lowering it with the gorilla’s blended prop support. The ankle stays attached while checked body angles fit the available space. During autonomous rage capture, other Oogas and short props already tagged as gorilla steps are ignored by the captive’s collision and support queries. The gorilla retains the existing support that lets it stride over those props; the held body may pass beneath or through them instead of stopping the pair. Terrain, structures and other gorillas remain solid. The autonomous launch and its airborne motion use the same Ooga/short-prop exemption, together with the decorative scenery filter above. A held Ooga retains its last strictly clear cancellation point. Ordinary movement and player-controlled grabs retain their collision rules.

Home-island throwing positions are a shared map of 96 fixed meadow points: 24 outward bearings on rings 8, 10, 12 and 14 m from the center. Each visit checks the map against the terrain and static scenery, with at most 24 stance or flight-segment checks per hub update, shared by all gorillas. Gates, glass and beds are checked in their solid state. Pile-layout changes that move lantern posts or restore scenery restart validation without changing spot IDs. A full-charge 45-degree launch envelope must clear the hills and structures and reach the sea without crossing back onto land or another island. Once the map is ready, a capture chooses the closest valid, non-rejected point. Selection scans the saved points; arrival and charging do not recalculate a flight. The actual release must fit the map's position, velocity and body envelope after the arm swing. This is a constant-time bounds check, including the gravity-first integration band at a 0.1-second frame. Raised support and an Ooga overhead remain valid when the release fits the same envelope.

The same map includes 48 rainforest points on four rings and eight bridge points: four centerline positions, each with a throw to either side. Capture records include height, so a supported bridge capture can select its own points without treating the deck as open air or classifying someone below it as a bridge walker. The flight still treats the water beside the bridge as outside land. Rainforest selection requires a direct dry route on the same side of the lake, and live sidesteps cannot enter swimming support while holding an Ooga. Its flight envelope includes the island's full voxel terrain and the bridge rails. Launch bounds are relative to each point's actual footing. A held body may extend over a bridge edge without needing ground beneath every limb; the hand attachment and real limb/structure sweeps remain required.

Candidate IDs remain fixed during a capture. A blocked movement or release tries the next nearest point while retaining G and the charge; exhausting the set waits three seconds before retrying. A new capture clears rejected points. Before the map is ready the gorilla retains its captive and charge. It runs directly to a selected point, with checked local sidesteps and jump or climb fallback; carrying does not use the pursuit grid. Arrival finishes within 0.15 m, turns outward and commits the rendered pose before starting the swing. Timechain and Bifrost retain their existing bounded coastline selection and full launch checks. Pursuit keeps the shared limit of 96 neighbor checks per frame. Difficult narrow corridors and routes from the other islands still need play-testing.

The frozen-frame report did not include an exception or profile proving its cause. Inspection found repeated full-flight mesh sweeps during arrival, charging and swing settling, multiplied by movement substeps. The home map removes those repeated sweeps from live rage; its initial bake is spread over updates instead of blocking a single frame. Debug data exposes map readiness, valid spots, the per-update check count and live full-flight proof count for the existing playthrough.

A thrown visitor can counter the launch with fueled jetpack steering. Horizontal thrust accelerates the actual launch velocity toward the requested flight velocity at 20 m/s²; it is integrated once, including the part of a frame after reaching that velocity. Without directional thrust, throw momentum remains ballistic. Exhausted fuel cannot accelerate the throw, and upward thrust does not clamp an already faster upward launch to the pack’s rise limit.

Nearby NPC Oogas flee raging gorillas through the existing fire-threat panic behavior, including its remembered escape direction and return to their previous activity. Detection starts within 8 m and fleeing continues until 10 m away. This includes bystanders, even though ordinary rage still pursues only its agitators. Driven, grabbed, stunned and thrown Oogas keep their existing controllers; rage alone never ignites an Ooga or starts drop-and-roll.

Rage reveals the Agent's original bright green voxel pattern, the same design referenced by 2140data's code-covered shell. `gorilla.setForm("code")` selects the rig's cached green palette and emissive details; inherited `matrixNative` mode preserves those colors inside the Matrix. The moving glyph overlay is disabled. Ending rage restores the ordinary ape palette and companion living mode while preserving fire, scorch and the global Matrix state.

Rage also owns the companion's behavior inside caves. EntropyLab cannot replace its quadruped stance with an upright scientist, apply the lab coat or doorway coat overlay, or resume a queued cave job during a jump or recovery. New work assignments wait until rage ends. Movement previews use the same quadruped as the rendered body, and ordinary cave behavior resumes after cancellation. Targets inside caves remain eligible under the usual pursuit and grab rules on either side of the border: relative foot height and the actual hand/body sweeps still prevent captures through walls or furniture. Cave interiors never use border teleport capture.

Oogas returning from the abyss respawn at random clear ground positions around the banana pile, including controlled visitors, thrown NPCs and NPCs falling during bed travel. The arrival ring follows the pile's platform radius, keeping bodies clear of the pile, scenery, fire and other characters. A bounded ring scan handles crowded random picks; if no clear position exists, respawn retries without falling back to a distant work or wandering slot. Possession, camera reset and existing abyss ammo loss are preserved.

## Cleanup and state priority

- Authentication, disposal, scene exit, health depletion and loss of eligibility cancel rage and its hit history. They stop pursuit and preparation immediately and release an unreleased captive.
- Possessing a gorilla cancels its autonomous rage. Releasing, changing or relocating a captured Ooga clears the paired capture record before normal control resumes.
- Fire, swimming and incompatible lifecycle changes retain their physical controllers and safely release any captive while the rage deadline keeps running. Checked jumps and climbs retain the captive and throw charge, then replan on landing. Rage cannot bypass their collision, grip, buoyancy or support requirements.
- Once a throw releases its Ooga, the launch belongs to the Ooga's existing physics. Later rage cancellation does not stop or repeat that flight.
- Captures are visit-local. Disposal restores the ordinary ape form, clears reservations and references, restores normal Ooga pose/control, and leaves no active autonomous throw behind. Contributor activity changes resume the companion's appropriate ordinary behavior.

## Future multiplayer handoff

The restriction is deliberate. Removing the account gate alone would let independent local gorillas capture bodies owned by different authorities. Multiplayer needs a separate design and acceptance pass:

| Concern | Decision and required behavior |
|---|---|
| Authority | Choose the authority for rage decisions, hit counts, target selection, capture, throw launch and health outcomes. Gorilla movement currently runs locally on each page; NPC Oogas follow the elected host, while a driven Ooga belongs to its player's page. Decide how those authorities agree before adding messages. |
| Hit validation | Attribute validated physical impacts to shooters with bounded, deduplicated event identities. Replayed `crew.showShot` events are visual-only and must remain unable to count again. Account for damage/headshots without turning damage amount into projectile count. |
| Capture ownership | Atomically reserve one captive per gorilla and one captor per Ooga across simultaneous attempts. Define a stable winner and rejection/release response, including stale or duplicated requests. |
| Player control | Coordinate capture acknowledgement and movement suppression with the victim's owning client. Preserve camera, character identity and ownership, and restore controls even when release arrives before capture acknowledgement. |
| Synchronization | Share rage expiry in an agreed clock, health cancellation, target, captive, animation phase and one-time throw launch state. Distinguish preparing a throw from its committed launch so snapshots cannot launch twice. |
| Recovery | Define safe release or authority transfer for latency, late joins, reconnects, host migration, either party disconnecting, scene changes and respawns. A recovered snapshot must neither freeze a captive nor repeat a throw. |
| Compatibility and cost | Bound messages, history and reservations. Define behavior for clients with different feature/protocol versions. Preserve the room's idle behavior: no periodic wakeups or timers while nobody is active. |

See [account and presence](auth-and-presence.md) for current host election, puppets, replay and the room's idle contract. No multiplayer rage protocol or synchronization is implemented by this change.
